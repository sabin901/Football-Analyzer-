# Three case studies to make this your portfolio

The dashboard is a tool. Your football judgement and communication are the portfolio. Author the interpretations yourself, identify AI assistance honestly, and retain reproducible evidence.

## 1. Opposition preparation — one page, five minutes

Pick the next opponent in a current league. In Match centre, select that club and download the last-five briefing notes. Check the included source links and the number of matches with each statistic. State three observations, then verify each against permissioned full-match video. Add timestamps and explain alternative causes such as score state, a dismissal or unusually strong opposition. End with two practical questions for the coaching staff; avoid prescribing a pressing plan from shot counts alone.

Deliver: a one-page briefing, source/sample note, video evidence you may share, and a five-minute presentation. Ask a coach what was useful and what did not change a decision.

## 2. Recruitment research — a role, not a leaderboard

Define the intended role first, for example a central midfielder expected to progress possession. In Event lab, choose a historical dataset, MID group and an appropriate minutes threshold. Compare progression, shot assists and recovery activity; include a player's negative or ambiguous evidence. Check whether a dataset follows one club rather than a complete league. Explain why possession share, team tactics, competition strength and estimated minutes limit the percentile comparison.

Deliver: a shortlist for further historical video review, not a current transfer recommendation. Describe a role-specific metric you would develop next and how you would validate it. Current squads and historical event identities are not joined by name.

## 3. Forecast evaluation — try to disprove your model

Download the prediction audit. Reproduce log loss against the frequency baseline and inspect one season separately. Confirm every training end precedes the test date. Investigate promoted teams, small venue samples and missing results. Write down a new model idea before evaluating it on a genuinely later holdout period. Keep a prospective journal of predictions published before kickoff; the current reconstructed backtest is not that journal.

Deliver: an experiment note with hypothesis, fixed evaluation design, baseline, metrics, uncertainty, failure cases and a decision about whether the added complexity is worthwhile.

## Engineering demonstration

Use `analysis_queries.sql` on the downloadable database. Show why the table grains and keys prevent accidental duplicate joins. Run the tests, trace a record to its source, simulate an unavailable provider, and explain the last-good behavior. Discuss the static hosting trade-off: inexpensive, cacheable and dependable, but scheduled rather than live and without private staff accounts or a multi-user annotation workflow.

This project is not a substitute for football-domain experience, licensed video/tracking skills, or professional software delivery. The next useful development should answer a real user's question, not merely add another metric.
